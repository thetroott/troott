1.0 Product Summary
<concise and informative overview of the feature >
We are building a version 0.1.0 of troott consisting of the customer application, the admin application (preachers and troott team), and the website.  Our customers interface with the sermons using the mobile app, the preachers/admin upload the sermons, the troott admin team manages both the customer and the preachers  apps with a monolith service while the website serves as a single source of information about troott. 

Problem Space

1.0 Product Opportunity
<should explain what user problem you’re solving and why the time is right to prioritize this product>
Many Christians find it hard to listen to both new and old sermons from their favorite preachers on their mobile phones anytime, anywhere  without struggling with unwanted Ads. It’s frustrating to search for specific content without knowing the exact title, and downloading sermons often leads to a disorganized mess on their phones.
On top of that, the current  platforms don’t offer a great mobile experience  for listening to sermons, making it harder than it should be for users to engage with the content they care about. The current value propositions offer poor experience (no mobile first experience) for our target users. 
Fixing these problems is key to delivering a seamless, enjoyable experience. By making it easier to find, organize, access  and listen to sermons, we can help users grow spiritually and stay more connected.
1.1 Problem Statement
<Contextual description of the user pain points and need>
To build this enhanced product experience, we asking the following questions:
How can we eliminate the frustration of people in searching, listening and sharing sermons on the go? 
How can we help people feel organised and free from the clutter of downloaded sermon collections to make more space for other items on their phones? ~ Allow users the freedom to carry their entire sermon collection in their pockets.
How can we help people gain control of their spiritual life by listening to sermons seamlessly so that they can improve their lifestyle?
How can we help people to share a common spiritual experience by sharing messages to their loved ones on their mobile device?

1.2 Pain-points and Frustrations 
<what headaches keep the target customer group(s) awake at night?>

Difficulty in accessing Old messages, it's challenging and annoying scrolling down on YouTube/Facebook pages to look for the messages - You have to know the Title of the message before you even search.
Inability to create personalised libraries of messages or teachings - this leads to not being able to come back to messages - download OR bookmark is underperforming in delivering this experience.
The difficulty and frustrations in discovering new preachers and teachings - especially if they are not heavily promoted or included popular playlists.
The lack of social features - Frustrated by the limited social features on existing products like YouTube, Spotify, - There is a desire for more interactive options to discover christian content through their friends or connect with others enthusiasts.
The annoyance of Frequent ads - Users find the presence of frequent ads on the free version of Spotify disruptive to their streaming experience.
Limited skips and offline listening - Free users have limitations on the number of skips they can use and are unable to download contents for offline listening, which can be frustrating when they want more control over their listening habits.

1.0 What are we building
<describe the ideas and solutions and how it will deliver value for the users>

troott is a subscription-based sermon and teaching streaming service, powered by AI. We're building a mobile app that makes it easy to find, listen to, and share sermons anytime, anywhere. No ads, no clutter—just a seamless way to grow spiritually.

We are developing a mobile app that makes it easy for users to access and engage with sermons anytime, anywhere. Our solution focuses on helping users grow spiritually by providing organized access to sermons, offering ad-free listening, and saving space on their devices. It also allows users to share content, deepen their faith, and build connections with others. This platform aims to deliver real value by making the sermon experience seamless, enjoyable, and more meaningful for users.

System design
ER DIagram

1️⃣ User opens the app and requests a song.
2️⃣ Backend (Node.js with Express/Fastify/NestJS) fetches song metadata (GraphQL/REST).
3️⃣ Backend checks available bitrates (FFmpeg-generated HLS/DASH files).
4️⃣ HLS/DASH stream is provided based on user's internet speed.
5️⃣ Node Streams API streams audio in real-time.
6️⃣ WebSockets update listener count in real-time.
7️⃣ Compressed API responses (Gzip/Brotli) optimize performance.

Christian ministers suffer from piracy and unmonetized content. Troott is the new mobile space for life-giving sermons and teachings, anytime, anywhere.  We are building a discipleship tool for the christian community using audio sermons.



Christianity is the largest faith in the world, over 2.3 billion people. More than 1.5 billion of them already have a phone and an internet connection. They’re not just reading scripture on paper anymore. They’re watching, listening, and sharing online. Faith-based media apps are downloaded over 150 million times every year, and that number is climbing. If you add up subscriptions, donations, and content licensing, the market is somewhere between $3 and $5 billion. We’re starting with the 50+ million digitally active Christians in Africa, North America, and diaspora communities, but the rest of the market is waiting.


Most pastors and Christian teachers give away their sermons for free, not because they want to, but because there’s no good way to distribute or monetize them. A lot of their work gets pirated, passed around with no credit, or buried on platforms where no one finds it. 


Our founding team works closely together like one product team. I’m the Full Stack Engineer, building the product from back to front and making sure it works well at scale. Our UX Designer focuses on understanding users and creating simple, engaging experiences. Our Data Engineer makes sure we collect sermons effectively and use vital data to guide our decisions. We solve problems together. Each of us has our own skills, but we share one goal: build a product Christians love to use every day.


We love sermons. For years, I’ve been hunting for them across WhatsApp groups, YouTube channels, and random blogs. It’s something we deal with every day, and it’s frustrating. There’s no single place to find the voices you trust. That’s why we’re building Troott. We know the pain, we know what good looks like, and we have the skills to make it happen. We’re set on fixing this, no matter what it takes.


We’ve built the first version of Troott and have eight ministers ready to use it. We’re in a private alpha, testing with a small group to make sure the experience works before opening it up. We already have 200 people on our waitlist, eager to join once we launch. The early feedback has been strong ministers see it as a way to reach more listeners and protect their content. This phase is about refining the product, learning from real usage, and preparing for a wider release.

In 6 months, we target 150 ministers, 20,000 sermon catalogue, 20,000 monthly active users, and 2,000 premium subscribers. We aim at 15% growth and 60% retention rate. That’s enough to prove the model works. In 3 years, we target 2500 ministers, 1.5m sermons, 5m+ users, and 1.5m premium subscribers.


We’re number-driven, using direct church onboarding and network effects from existing connections. Growth starts invite-only, with “share an invite” rewards for subscription points. We’ll gamify sermon discovery, leverage virality, and measure relentlessly to compound acquisition, retention, and monetization toward our 3-year targets.
